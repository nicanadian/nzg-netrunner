WHAT THE EVALUATION COVERS

Rules first. Presentation second.

The replay is generated through the real engine: failed run, multi-access, agenda steal and score, breaker strength, broken subroutine, successful asset trash, tag and tag removal. Tests regenerate the artifact and independently replay all decisions.

The browser comparison measures root/ice geometry, order, HQ identity, clipped cards, board scrolling, card sizes, loaded assets and hidden-card privacy. Visual reviews inform the next revision; there is no invented aesthetic score.

Responsive lossless table images replace the older JPEG faces. Inspection uses genuine 743px card crops from NSG’s official 300ppi print-and-play PDF, preserving its printed credits. Native names remain readable when printed card text is too small; hover and pinned inspection provide the full card and selectable rules text.

Substitutions and limits

The viewer steps through recorded positions. Choose “Play live” to play a normal shuffled game against the AI. Recent sets, HQ damage and exact video credit totals are outside this adaptation. Gwent informs card emphasis and feedback; these checks do not establish parity with its production quality.

THE HILL CLIMB

Keep changes that survive comparison.

  1. Archive: preserve the original frontend, then render the same engine DTO in both versions.
  2. Correct: anchor HQ, reverse the root/ice relationship and preserve true inner-to-outer order.
  3. Inspect: the first revision cropped outer ice at laptop resolution. Reclaim header space, compact empty zones and remove repeated lane footers.
  4. Measure again: the final tested checkpoints fit without board scrolling or clipped cards at both desktop sizes; installed Runner cards grew from 90px to 100–108px.

Dense late-game boards retain internal scrolling. Mobile uses a scrolling table and stacked controls; this comparison does not imply that every possible board fits one screen.
