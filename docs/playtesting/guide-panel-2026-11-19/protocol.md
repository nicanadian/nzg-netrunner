# Guide-led deckbuilding panel · 19 November 2026

Three independent AI reviewers use the actual shared browser. These are simulated player perspectives, not recruited humans. Their subjective scores are directional critique, not a human user-study result or competitive balance measurement.

Each reviewer must open the lobby's Pairing & learning guide, choose a matchup, explain a small legal deck revision, make it through rendered deckbuilder controls, select the saved revision and complete a browser game. Public state may inform strategy; hidden engine state and direct game-action injection are prohibited. Bounded DOM-button scripts may handle repetitive choices, with that method disclosed. Session data may be read at completion to preserve replay evidence.

Reviewers cover a Gateway-graduate Runner, a Corp learner, and an experienced tinkerer exploring a difficult matchup. Each saves an independent review, exact deck document, interaction notes and full replay. The parent validates the replay and legal construction before combining findings. The six/54-game guide records describe bundled lists; no tester should transfer those estimates to custom revisions or claim improvement from one game.

Test tabs use the incognito profile. The existing default-profile game and saved decks are untouched. The parent captures incognito storage before testing and restores it after every reviewer has finished. No source changes, release, or publishing is part of this review task.

Feedback questions:

- Can the guide help a player choose an appropriate learning goal and opponent?
- Does it teach enough to make a deliberate deck change? Where is additional rules or deckbuilding help needed?
- Are the saved deck, legality, influence and actual game matchup clear?
- What choices feel engaging, repetitive, confusing or unresponsive during play?
- Which observations are reproducible bugs, guide gaps, strategic mistakes or subjective preferences?
