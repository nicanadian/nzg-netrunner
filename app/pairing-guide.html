Build one small experiment

  1. Start with a named list. Choose one learning goal and keep a baseline to compare against.
  2. Change one or two slots. Say what you gain and what you give up. Keep income, ice/breaker coverage and any required support.
  3. Check construction. The builder shows your identity’s minimum size and influence budget. Own-faction cards use zero influence; imports use the printed amount per copy. Agendas must be in-faction or neutral. Usually at most three copies of a card; check its own limit. Unique limits active copies, not copies in a deck; console limits are separate.
  4. Check whether the plan still works. Legal does not mean every ability has support. Keep a way to pay restricted costs, enough memory, and a plan for each ice type.
  5. Save a revision, then observe it. A custom list has no measured matchup rate here. A win alone does not test your edit.
Worked Corp repair: size, influence and agendas

A 44-card Corp deck needs 18–19 agenda points. At 45 cards it needs 20–21: adding a non-agenda can change the band. Required points are 2 × floor(deck size ÷ 5) + 2, or one more. In our Reality Plus example, adding three Anoetic Voids produced 47 cards, 17/15 influence and only 18 agenda points. Removing two Voids and one Retribution left 44 cards, 8/15 influence and 18 agenda points. This illustrates a legal repair, not a stronger deck.

Returning to a saved deck or game

Use My decks & imports to edit a saved list. The editor compares it to its recorded baseline across revisions. Menu → Change decks returns to setup. Save & use selects your revised list for a new game. Resume restores the original game-start snapshot; Jack in starts with your current setup. View matchup decks during play shows that game’s original lists, even if the library now has a revision with the same name.

How to read the results

  • Matchup rows switch to the selected player side’s test-win rate. Raw Runner/Corp counts stay visible.
  • 40–60%: close results in this sample—a pairing to try, rather than proof of balance.
  • Below 40%: harder for your selected side in these tests. Above 60%: harder for the opposing side.
  • 54 games per pairing: expanded tests for the recommended Vantage decks. Six games: preliminary results for Gateway, Elevation and interaction pilots; one game shifts the percentage by about 17 points.
  • Results describe our AI playing both sides, not human tournament strength. Different lists for the same identity can behave differently.
  • With a friend, swap sides and try a few games before judging a pairing. For learning, familiarity with the cards matters as much as the win record.
  • Custom decks have not been benchmarked here. Use the exact bundled deck names shown above.
